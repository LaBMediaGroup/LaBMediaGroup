# Wildlife at the east field edge

One or two does can come out of the eastern tree line, stop to graze in a break in the corn and walk home. A small fraction of visits reach the eastern pond bank. Most of the time the field is quiet. A nearby approach can turn a grazing visit into a retreat. The second doe can follow the first. Within earshot of a calm Labcoon, a grazing doe can pause with her head up. These detection distances and encounter odds are scene tuning. The [Pennsylvania Game Commission](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/pgc/wildlife/discover-pa-wildlife/white-tailed-deer) describes alertness to motion and sound and the use of fruit and acorns. The [Missouri field guide](https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/white-tailed-deer) provides photographs, adult proportions and the seasonal coat.

The doe uses Quaternius’s [Deer](https://poly.pizza/m/T6Cs7tmMHJ), distributed under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). The compact embedded GLB keeps five native clips and seven skinned material surfaces. It has no external textures. Render scale .4 gives a roughly 1.76 m body along the asset's long axis. The smaller companion is 94.5% of that size. The head faces +Z in the pond. Both coats are matte gray-brown. Walking hooves are grounded against the terrain. A full-frame posed mesh check on level ground measured the lowest galloping hoof at −0.0452 m and the highest lowest hoof at +0.1482 m before the runtime lift.

The common green darner is an original lightweight pond model. The green thorax, adjoining eyes, narrow segmented abdomen, four transparent wings and folded legs follow the National Park Service photographs. The body is about .071 m long and the wings span roughly .095 m. Adults patrol warm water and take insects on the wing. A calm pass can come close to Labcoon, while a disturbance can send one sideways. Activity in this scene requires mild daylight conditions between May and October. [National Park Service species account and photographs](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/common-green-darner.htm)

The local brain owns encounters on the real clock. The viewer reads trajectories from the owner reader, including while Labcoon is elsewhere. The two copies of `wildlife-motion.js` in the pond and the brain must match. Both use metres and Unix milliseconds.

The four wings pivot at their individual thorax attachments and counterstroke during hovering. They come briefly closer in phase when the insect darts. A roughly 35 Hz wingbeat, small frequency variation, pitching at stroke reversal and a few translucent shutter samples keep fast movement readable at ordinary display rates. Reduced-motion mode holds a steady wing pose. The timing is an artistic approximation for this green darner. Wang and Russell measured a mean of 33.4 Hz in a different species, *Libellula pulchella*.
