**Revised Nov 18:** Bobby moved the story generator back to fridge magnets, and the projector now shows the films. The original pitch follows.

**Why a projector, not the fridge (first pitch).** A projector is a filmmaker's object, and it makes the idea out of light. That lets the story tool show off lighting too: the beam has dust, the lens has bloom, and the logline lands soft-edged on the plaster. A fridge with magnets says "kitchen". A projector says "pitch meeting".

## A: Late Light Loft (chosen)

A real working studio in a converted Michigan industrial loft. There is a tall steel-framed west window and limewashed brick and plaster walls. The floor is worn white-oak plank, with exposed fir beams overhead, a rug, a long desk and a cinema camera on sticks by the window.

- **Light**: physically placed sun (NOAA solar position for Shelby Township) through a deep window reveal. Mullion shadows rake across the floor and climb the north wall as the sun drops. There is a warm bounce off the floor patch and dust in the beam. Practicals (desk lamp, string bulb) fade up after sunset. Blue hour cools the room.
- **Camera**: a fluid-head pan with a hint of dolly, like a person turning in the room. No cuts, no logo flight.
- **Portrait**: the window is tall (floor-to-beam) so a phone frames it vertically with the floor patch below. The calendar wall stacks calendar over the desk.

## B: Set Model

The studio as a 1:12 foam-core and balsa set maquette, cut away on a black stage under a single work light. A snorkel-lens camera moves through the model, with tilt-shift depth of field. Tools are miniature props, and the "sun" is a lamp on an articulated arm.

- Shows the model-making and animation craft, and portrait cutaways come naturally.
- But it is a model of a studio, not a studio. Light on a miniature reads as a desk lamp, not golden hour, and the brief asks for one believable room.

## Pick: A

The brief asks for a *believable* studio where the sun moves across the room. A does that literally and gives the lighting the most to do. B's maquette look can come back later as an easter egg: pull the camera all the way out and the loft turns out to be a model on a bench.
