Fourteen pages, one stylesheet.

This site has no framework or build step. It’s hand-written HTML and CSS on free hosting, plus one small Cloudflare Worker that protects the assistant’s optional AI phrasing. If you’re a filmmaker who also tinkers, this is the part you might find useful.

One stylesheet, everything

lab.css is 1,096 lines (47 KB) and governs all fourteen pages : every colour, every typeface, the navigation and the footer. Change a value in the token block and it updates across the entire site at once.

Seventy-one design tokens cover colour, type, spacing and motion. Four themes : dark, light, sepia and forest : are per-theme overrides of the same tokens, applied by an inline script in the <head> before first paint so there's no flash of the wrong colours.

What needs JavaScript, and what doesn't

The Reel and SkyBound are plain HTML. Production notes, timelines and all 81 behind-the-scenes photographs remain readable with JavaScript switched off.

The notebook pages, gear list, Story Generator and LaB Assistant are built from shared data files. JavaScript handles rendering, search, saved kits and live counts; each page includes a plain <noscript> explanation instead of leaving an empty shell.

That trade keeps 126 resources, 66 gear items and 1,334 prompts in one source of truth. Videos are separate: local posters load immediately, and the youtube-nocookie.com player is created only after Play is pressed.

Details worth stealing

None of these are clever. They're the small decisions that separate a site that works from one that only looks like it does: and every one of them is a few lines you could lift into your own thing this afternoon.

01

A frame that doesn’t flinch when you press play

The reel is a 2.39:1 film exported inside a 16:9 YouTube frame, so the black bars are baked into the video. The poster hides them with object-fit:cover, and the player used to expose them by opening out to 16:9: the frame jumped taller and the bars appeared. Now it doesn’t move at all: the iframe is sized to 16:9 by width: 134.44% of the container’s height: and centred, so what overflows top and bottom is exactly 7.205% of the width each side. The true letterbox on a 2.39 film is 7.204%. It crops to the pixel.

The catch is that YouTube draws its own furniture in those bars: the title overlay, the “Watch on YouTube” strip and the control bar: so cropping them means replacing them. There’s a small bar of our own instead: pause, close, and a fullscreen button that hands over to the native player, where the full 16:9 frame and the real scrubber are waiting. Close destroys the iframe rather than hiding it, so playback genuinely stops. Worth knowing if you ever embed scope footage: the letterbox is in the file, not in the page.

02

Nothing contacts YouTube until you press play

Every video is a poster image and a button. The youtube-nocookie.com iframe is injected on click and not before, so simply reading a page sets no cookies and loads no tracking script. It is also dramatically faster, because a page with six films embeds zero players until you want one.

The catch, which took an outside pair of eyes to spot: the facade only works if the poster is yours. Pull it from i.ytimg.com and Google is contacted on page load anyway: the player is deferred, the surveillance isn’t. Host the frames locally, and drop any preconnect to the same host, or the whole exercise is decoration.

03

A finger cannot hover

106 photographs on this site are grayscale until you hover them, which is the entire visual idea. On a touchscreen that reveal can never fire. Under @media (hover:none) the photographs simply arrive in colour instead.

Worth checking on anything where hover carries meaning rather than decoration. Touchscreen devices receive full colour automatically so nothing is hidden behind an impossible gesture.

04

Colour solved numerically, and per theme

Every text color was calibrated to clear WCAG AA 4.5:1 contrast against every theme background, then tested and approved by human eyes across every device. The tertiary grey is #857F76 because that is the value that passes comfortably on both dark and light surfaces.

Contrast-sensitive colors use per-theme tokens so that accent tones remain vibrant and perfectly legible whether viewing in dark, light, sepia, or forest themes. Mathematical accuracy paired with human visual verification keeps every page clean and effortless to read.

05

Animations that cost nothing when idle

Hover the line at the bottom of any page. The hearts are five elements sharing one keyframe, each handed its own drift and delay as a custom property so none of them move in lockstep.

They ship animation-play-state: paused and only run while you're actually there: no loop spinning behind an invisible layer, and nothing at all under prefers-reduced-motion.

06

Native elements before libraries

Every expandable section: the production timelines, the gear categories, the resource groups: is a plain <details> and <summary>. No accordion library, no JavaScript, and keyboard support, screen-reader semantics and find-in-page all work for free.

Browsers will even open a collapsed <details> to show a search match inside it, which no hand-rolled accordion does.

07

Share links keyed by content, not position

A brief you send someone is encoded as a short hash of its own text, never its position in the list. So the prompt library can grow or be reordered entirely, and a link already out in the world still opens the same brief. Saved kits on the gear page work the same way.

Index-based links would have silently rotted the first time the data changed. Keying by hash keeps every shared link stable forever.

08

The one page nobody plans to visit

The zero on the missing page is an eye: drawn, not typeset, because a font’s counter isn’t knowable from CSS and Space Mono’s zero has a mark inside it that read as a second pupil. One path with fill-rule: evenodd makes the counter a real hole, so the page shows through in any theme and the pupil stays clipped inside the letter.

The eye follows your cursor, wanders when you sit still, and gives up on you entirely if you poke at it five times. The message rerolls from two pools: one is funny about the page being gone, the other is filmmaking encouragement.

09

A chatbot with search in charge

The assistant searches the site’s own film notes, 126 resources, 66 gear entries, dated events and quiet field notes before it writes a word. Every factual answer keeps a source. Field notes are authored retrieval records, not model training and not extra page copy: they hold exact filenames, settings, production memories and occasional Easter eggs. Exact field-note answers bypass model rewriting so filenames and paths come back untouched. Follow an internal citation and an orange guide moves to the exact film, resource, event or item of kit.

On the public site, a protected Cloudflare Worker can hand the retrieved answer to a fixed model for a natural voice. The raw visitor question is omitted from that cloud request; only the already-sourced public answer is sent for phrasing. A grounding check rejects any rewrite that drops or changes a date or claim. The model can change the phrasing; it cannot change the facts.

What it doesn't do

  • No advertisements, banners, or sponsored content anywhere.
  • No cookies, and therefore no consent banner.
  • No accounts, no sign-up, no email capture anywhere.
  • No affiliate links. Nothing on the gear page was sent free.
  • Saved kits and prompts live in your own browser and are never sent anywhere.
  • Every animation stops under prefers-reduced-motion.

There is one quiet counter worth stating plainly: a lightweight visitor counter, GoatCounter. It is open source, sets no cookies, stores no personal data and doesn't identify you across sites. It simply counts page visits so we know if people read the site, without putting a banner in front of you. The numbers are at labmedia.goatcounter.com if you want to look.

If you want to build one

The honest version: it is plain HTML and CSS, and the hard parts were never the code. They were deciding what to cut, and then actually checking rather than assuming: two pages and four resource entries came out of this rebuild for not earning their place. Though “not the code” doesn’t mean the code is nothing.

Hosting is free and the domain is the only cost. The free tools on the resources list are where I’d start. Have a go first, then if you get stuck on something specific, ask me. That offer is real, it is just worth a great deal more once you have hit the wall yourself.
